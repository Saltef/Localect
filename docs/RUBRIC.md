# Rating sheet instructions

You are reading replies a small shop might send to a customer who wrote in
your language. Each row shows the customer's message and two candidate
replies, **left** and **right**. You do not know where either came from, and
the order is random. Judge only what is on the page.

Rate one language: open only the sheet for your locale (`sheet-ar-EG.csv`
and so on). Put your name or a code in the `rater` column of every row you
fill, or rename the file `sheet-ar-EG.<yourname>.csv`. Fill as many rows as
you can; a half-filled sheet is still useful. Leave a cell empty rather
than guess.

Columns to fill:

| column | what to write |
|---|---|
| prefer | `left`, `right`, or `tie`: which reply you would rather receive as this customer |
| nativeness_left / _right | 1 to 5: 1 = obviously translated or foreign, 3 = correct but not how we say it, 5 = a native from this region wrote it |
| register_left / _right | 1 to 5: 1 = wrong tone for a shop writing to a customer here (too stiff, too casual, wrong form of address), 5 = exactly right |
| facts_left / _right | `y` or `n`: does the reply answer what was asked without adding or changing a fact? Judge against the message only |
| send_left / _right | `y` or `n`: would you send this as is, without editing a word? |
| edit | optional: the one change you would make, in your language |

What to ignore: the greeting line and the sign-off. They are fixed by the
shop's settings and are the same on both sides. Judge the body.

What not to do: do not reward length, do not reward formality for its own
sake, do not penalize a reply for refusing something the customer had no
right to ask for. If both are bad, say which is less bad and score both low.

Time: about one minute per row. Stop when tired; agreement between raters
drops with fatigue and we measure that.
